Ranking rules

How we calculate the ranking

Chess, physics and cooking measure very different skills. These rules let us compare learners across all of them fairly.

The rankings still show sample players while we finish the platform. Once real results arrive, they follow exactly these rules.

Two numbers: app rating and EduDB score

  • The app rating shows how strong you are inside one app. Every app has its own.
  • The EduDB score compares you across apps. It is built from your place in each app, not from the rating numbers themselves.

1. The rating inside each app

  • The developers of each app decide its rating formula, because they know their subject best. We put that formula on the EduDB server.
  • Your rating is calculated only on the EduDB server. Nobody can change it from the browser, and the same rules apply to everyone.
  • An app without its own formula uses the standard one: everyone starts at 1000, a correct answer raises the rating and a wrong one lowers it. A hard question gives more for a correct answer than an easy one.
  • Only learning in the apps changes a rating. Teachers, schools and achievements never do.

2. Who counts in an app

  • You have answered at least 30 questions in the app, so your rating has settled.
  • You were active in the app during the last 90 days.
  • The app has at least 10 such players. A smaller app does not give a score yet.

3. Your score in an app

Your score in an app depends on two things: how many of its players you are ahead of, and how big the app is.

Score in an app = share × weight × 100

Share

The share of the app's players you are ahead of. First place is 100%, the middle of the table is 50%, the last place is 0%.

Weight

A bigger app counts more, because being ahead of 900 people is harder than being ahead of 90. The weight is the number of digits in the player count (exactly: the decimal logarithm of it).

Players in the appWeight
10 1
100 2
1,000 3
10,000 4

The weight grows slowly on purpose. Ten times more players add only one step, so a big app counts more but never pushes out the smaller ones.

Examples

StudentAppPlaceShareWeightScore
Anna Chess, 1,000 players top 10% 90%3 270
Bohdan Physics, 100 players top 10% 90%2 180
Daria Physics, 100 players 1st place 100%2 200
Ivan Chess, 1,000 players middle 50%3 150

The top 10% of an app with 1,000 players (270) rank above the top 10% of an app with 100 players (180). Even first place in the smaller app (200) stays below them.

4. Category, overall and school rankings

Category score
The sum of your scores in the apps of that category.
Overall score
The sum of your scores in all apps. Being strong in several apps adds up.
School score
The average overall score of the school members who have one. A big school does not win just by its size.
This week
How much your overall score grew since last Monday.

5. Fair play

  • Scores are recalculated every night from the ratings of all players.
  • The server ignores repeated answers and too many answers in a short time.
  • A person banned from a school by its director no longer counts in that school's ranking.
  • Achievements from apps, teachers and schools appear on your profile. They never change a rating or a score.
